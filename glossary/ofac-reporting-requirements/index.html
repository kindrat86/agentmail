OFAC Reporting Requirements

Legal obligations to report blocked property, rejected transactions, and sanctions matches to OFAC within specified timeframes.

TL;DR

TL;DR: OFAC requires reporting of blocked property, rejected transactions, and other sanctions-related events within specified timeframes. These filings create the paper trail OFAC uses to assess compliance.

What must be reported

Two events drive most reporting. Blocked property, where funds or assets are frozen because they involve a sanctioned party, must be reported to OFAC, typically within 10 business days, with details of the blocked party and the assets. Rejected transactions, where a payment is refused before processing, are reported through separate channels. Some programs also require reports of specific matches or attempted transactions.

Why the paper trail matters

Reports are not just administrative. They tell OFAC what your organization touched, when, and what it did about it. Complete, timely reports show an active compliance program. Gaps, late filings, or reports that never arrive suggest the opposite and compound whatever the underlying problem was.

How screening connects

Every screening result is a potential reporting event. A clean result is not reported. A flagged result that leads to a rejection or a block is. That means the screening system and the reporting process need to feed each other, so that nothing is frozen or refused without a corresponding record.
