OFAC Non-Proliferation Sanctions

Sanctions targeting weapons of mass destruction proliferators, their support networks, and entities involved in missile technology transfer.

TL;DR

TL;DR: Non-proliferation sanctions block the people and entities behind weapons of mass destruction programs and missile technology. Screening catches both the proliferators and the networks that support them.

Who these sanctions target

Non-proliferation programs designate the individuals and entities involved in the spread of weapons of mass destruction, along with those who provide support and those engaged in missile technology transfer. The designations are list-based, so they flow onto the SDN List, and a payment to a designated proliferator or facilitator is prohibited.

Why it matters for agent payments

Proliferation networks use layers of intermediaries precisely to avoid standing out, so a payment platform has no reliable way to spot them by appearance. Screening each counterparty against the current list is the control that catches these parties before a transfer completes. An agent that screens every payment closes the path these networks would otherwise use to receive funds.

What screening catches

  • Designated proliferators and facilitators
  • Entities involved in missile technology transfer
  • Associated wallets and aliases

Why intermediaries matter

Intermediaries matter because proliferation networks are built to hide the end user. A payment to a front company or a trading intermediary may look ordinary while ultimately supporting a prohibited program. Screening catches the designated intermediary, but catching the pattern requires checking each counterparty in the chain, not just the final one. An agent that screens every leg of a payment is harder to route funds through.
