OFAC Belarus Sanctions Screening

Screening under the targeted sanctions on Belarusian government officials and state-owned enterprises, expanded after the 2020 election and support for Russia's actions in Ukraine.

TL;DR

TL;DR: Belarus sanctions target government officials and state-owned enterprises, with the program expanded after the 2020 election. Screening catches counterparties linked to those listed parties.

What the program targets

Belarus sanctions are targeted at specific officials and state-owned enterprises rather than the country as a whole. The program expanded after the 2020 election and again in connection with support for Russia's actions in Ukraine. Because many targets are enterprises, ownership and control links matter, and the 50 Percent Rule can extend a designation to entities owned by listed parties.

Why it matters for agent payments

A counterparty tied to a listed Belarusian state enterprise can be blocked even if the counterparty's own name never appears on a list. An agent should screen the name, the wallet, and the ownership chain against current SDN data before paying. Screening before the transfer is the only point where the payment can be stopped cleanly.

What to check

  • Counterparty against SDN entries
  • Ownership links to listed enterprises
  • Destination wallets

State-linked counterparties

State-linked counterparties are the main screening challenge for Belarus, because the targets are often enterprises rather than individuals. A company with a Belarusian state owner can be blocked through the 50 Percent Rule even if it trades under an innocuous name. Resolving beneficial ownership is therefore part of the check, and an agent should screen ownership links alongside names and wallets before approving a payment to a Belarus-linked counterparty.
