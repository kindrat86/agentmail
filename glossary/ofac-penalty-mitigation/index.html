OFAC Penalty Mitigation

The factors that can reduce an OFAC civil penalty, including voluntary self-disclosure, cooperation, an existing compliance program, and prompt remedial action.

TL;DR

TL;DR: OFAC penalty mitigation is the set of factors that lower the fine after a sanctions violation. Disclosing a violation yourself, cooperating with the investigation, and fixing the gap are the most reliable ways to reduce what you owe.

What penalty mitigation means

OFAC weighs both aggravating and mitigating factors when it sets a civil penalty. Mitigation does not erase a violation, but it can meaningfully reduce the dollar amount. The strongest mitigating factors are a voluntary self-disclosure made before OFAC detects the problem, substantial cooperation during the investigation, a sanctions compliance program that existed before the violation, and remedial steps taken immediately after it was found.

Why it matters for agent payments

AI agents move money without a human in the loop, so a mistake can happen fast and silently. If an agent pays a flagged counterparty, the penalty discussion shifts to what the operator did about it. Keeping screening logs and an audit trail of every ALLOW or BLOCK decision gives evidence of a functioning program, which is exactly the kind of record OFAC treats as mitigating.

Mitigating factors to document

  • Voluntary self-disclosure before OFAC inquiry
  • Cooperation during the investigation
  • A compliance program in place before the violation
  • Remedial action, such as adding screening to the payment path

How mitigation and aggravation balance

OFAC does not apply mitigation in isolation. The agency first identifies aggravating conduct, then weighs the mitigating factors against it. A company that discloses late, cooperates poorly, and only builds a program after the fact will see little reduction. The strongest outcomes come from a program that existed before the violation, disclosure that came promptly, and remediation that closed the specific gap. For an agent operator, that means the screening check and its logs should exist before the first payment, not after a problem surfaces.
