OFAC Enforcement Guidelines

The rules published at 31 CFR Part 501 Appendix A that OFAC uses to decide whether a violation occurred and how to calculate a penalty.

TL;DR

TL;DR: The enforcement guidelines are the public rulebook OFAC follows when it investigates and penalizes sanctions violations. They set out aggravating and mitigating factors and the penalty ranges OFAC can apply.

What the guidelines cover

Published at 31 CFR Part 501 Appendix A, the guidelines describe how OFAC determines whether a violation happened and how it sizes the penalty. They distinguish willful from non-willful conduct, list the factors that raise or lower a fine, and explain how voluntary self-disclosure and cooperation are credited. Because OFAC operates under strict liability, intent is not required for a violation, but it heavily influences the penalty.

Why it matters for agent payments

Civil penalties for OFAC violations start at $356,000 per violation, so the difference between aggravation and mitigation is real money. For an agent that moves funds automatically, the guidelines make it clear that documented controls, prompt disclosure, and a working screening step are the difference between a reduced penalty and a larger one.

Key concepts in the guidelines

  • Strict liability: intent is not required for a violation
  • Aggravating factors that raise the penalty
  • Mitigating factors such as disclosure and cooperation

Aggravating versus mitigating in the guidelines

The guidelines describe a spectrum rather than a single number. OFAC starts from the violation, applies aggravating factors such as willfulness and management awareness, then applies mitigating factors such as disclosure and remediation, and arrives at a penalty within the statutory range. Because the range is wide, the same conduct can produce very different outcomes depending on how the company responded. The guidelines are therefore as much about behavior after the violation as the violation itself.
