What Dow Jones documents about the database

Dow Jones positions Risk & Compliance as compliance data for anti-money-laundering, bribery, corruption, and sanctions obligations, aimed at compliance teams rather than developers. Its own Risk & Compliance site describes analyzing data on high-risk individuals and entities to meet those obligations.

Scale is documented in Dow Jones's 2024 Data Quality Report: the consolidated profile database grew from 530,000 profiles in 2008 to 4.23 million in July 2024, up from 3.99 million in July 2023. The report also documents update cadence: all sanctions data is consolidated into the flagship Watchlist database and updated every 24 hours via an XML or CSV download, while the premium Lists for Payments & Transaction Screening (LPTS) feed updates up to six times a day, at intervals as narrow as four hours.

Delivery is documented on the data feeds and APIs page: feeds and APIs that plug into customer workflows for onboarding, monitoring, and investigation. Adverse media content is built on Factiva licensed news, which Dow Jones describes as 33,000+ sources in 33 languages across 200 countries. Sanctions data quality has been reviewed by an external ISAE 3000 audit for seven consecutive years, per Dow Jones's press release.

One integration constraint is documented in the developer portal: feed updates are capped at 12,500 profiles per day per major content set (Watchlist, State Owned Companies, Adverse Media Entities, Marijuana-Related Businesses, Money Service Businesses, and Military End Use Associated Entities). If your pipeline mirrors the daily delta, that cap is part of your design math. See the Daily Update Limits reference.

What the official pages do not publish

On the pages SanctionsAI reviewed - the Risk & Compliance overview, the product pages, and the developer portal - there is no public price list, no package table, and no self-serve checkout. The developer portal's compliance dataset description ends its source-list note with "Request a Trial", which is the documented self-serve entry point, and the product pages route buyers to sales contact.

That evidence supports a narrow conclusion: a buyer needs a vendor quote. It does not support an estimate of the contract value. Quote scope can depend on which content sets you license, whether you consume feeds, APIs, or hosted RiskCenter seats, update frequency, historical archive depth, and implementation support. SanctionsAI does not repeat unsourced marketplace estimates or present an "industry average" as if it were Dow Jones's price.

Correction (3 October 2026): an earlier version of this page described Dow Jones Risk & Compliance as having World-Check lineage. That was wrong. World-Check is an LSEG Risk Intelligence database: founded as World-Check, acquired by Thomson Reuters in 2011, transferred to Refinitiv in 2018, and now under LSEG. Dow Jones Risk & Compliance is a separate, competing database operated by Dow Jones (News Corp). Comparison pages that blend the two lineages should be read with that distinction in mind.

SanctionsAI quote checklist

Send the same written requirements to every vendor on your shortlist. That makes the returned proposals comparable and exposes exclusions before implementation begins.

  • Content sets: name the sets your workflow needs - Watchlist (sanctions), PEP, Adverse Media Entities, State Owned Companies, and specialist sets - rather than licensing a broad claim.
  • Delivery method: feeds (XML/CSV delta downloads), APIs, hosted screening tools, or analyst seats. These are different deliverables with different cost structures.
  • Update frequency: the 24-hour Watchlist cadence and the 4-hour LPTS cadence are documented differently - price the one your risk window requires.
  • Volume definition: what counts as a screened name, how rescreens and monitoring hits are counted, and what happens above the allowance.
  • Archive depth: historical coverage (Dow Jones documents PEP archives back to 2001) matters for back-testing and audits.
  • Audit evidence: ask which ISAE 3000 report period you can review and under what NDA.
  • Implementation: separate subscription charges from setup, integration, and internal engineering.
  • Trial: define the trial dataset, production restrictions, and whether a trial converts automatically.

How SanctionsAI would evaluate the trial

A trial should test the decision path, not the interface. Use a controlled set of known benign, sanctioned, and ambiguous names your compliance team is permitted to test, and record whether results are explainable and reproducible, how uncertain matches are presented, and what evidence is available for review. Do not infer production cost from trial access. Ask Dow Jones to turn the tested workflow into a written proposal stating the content sets, delivery method, update cadence, usage measure, and all material exclusions.

Dow Jones and SanctionsAI are not like-for-like purchases

Dow Jones Risk & Compliance is an enterprise risk-data platform: consolidated profiles, Factiva-backed adverse media, feed and API delivery, and analyst tooling for compliance teams. The narrow scope is useful when the requirement is a lightweight sanctions signal inside an automated path. It does not replace a consolidated risk database, adverse media screening, or a regulated institution's broader compliance program.
