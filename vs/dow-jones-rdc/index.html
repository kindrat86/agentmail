SanctionsAI vs Dow Jones RDC (Risk & Compliance)

Dow Jones Risk & Compliance (RDC) is an enterprise risk-data platform sold to compliance teams. SanctionsAI is a narrow OFAC sanctions screening API aimed at developers and AI agents. This page compares the two on documented facts - what Dow Jones publishes about its database, what it does not publish, and which tool fits which job - as reviewed on 9 October 2026.

SanctionsAI answer: if the job is a sanctions decision inside an automated payment or onboarding path, SanctionsAI is the direct fit: a REST API that returns JSON and that you can call from a plain curl, free for 5 checks a day with no signup or API key. If the job is enterprise risk coverage - a consolidated profile database, adverse media, PEP data, and audit evidence for a regulated compliance program - Dow Jones RDC is built for exactly that, and SanctionsAI does not replace it. The two are not like-for-like purchases, and the honest comparison starts with the decision your system has to make.

What Dow Jones documents about Risk & Compliance

Dow Jones positions Risk & Compliance as compliance data for anti-money-laundering, bribery, corruption, and sanctions obligations. Its Risk & Compliance site describes analyzing data on high-risk individuals and entities to meet those obligations, and the product overview is written for compliance teams rather than developers.

Scale is documented in Dow Jones's 2024 Data Quality Report: the consolidated profile database grew from 530,000 profiles in 2008 to 4.23 million in July 2024, up from 3.99 million in July 2023. The same report documents update cadence: all sanctions data is consolidated into the flagship Watchlist database and updated every 24 hours via an XML or CSV download, while the premium Lists for Payments & Transaction Screening (LPTS) feed updates up to six times a day, at intervals as narrow as four hours.

Delivery is documented on the data feeds and APIs page: feeds and APIs that plug into customer workflows for onboarding, monitoring, and investigation. Adverse media content is built on Factiva licensed news, which Dow Jones describes as 33,000+ sources in 33 languages across 200 countries. Sanctions data quality has been reviewed by an external ISAE 3000 audit for seven consecutive years, per Dow Jones's press release.

One integration constraint is documented in the developer portal: feed updates are capped at 12,500 profiles per day per major content set (Watchlist, State Owned Companies, Adverse Media Entities, Marijuana-Related Businesses, Money Service Businesses, and Military End Use Associated Entities). If your pipeline mirrors the daily delta, that cap belongs in your design math. See the Daily Update Limits reference.

What the official pages do not publish

On the pages SanctionsAI reviewed on 9 October 2026 - the Risk & Compliance overview, the product pages, and the developer portal - there is no public price list, no package table, and no self-serve checkout. The developer portal's compliance dataset description points requests for a detailed source list to a "Request a Trial" action, and the product pages route buyers to sales contact. The documented evidence supports a narrow conclusion: a buyer needs a vendor quote. It does not support an estimate of the contract value, and this page does not repeat unsourced marketplace estimates. Where each is the better fit

Choose Dow Jones RDC when the requirement is breadth. A consolidated multi-million-profile database, Factiva-backed adverse media in 33 languages, specialist content sets, and seven years of externally audited data-quality evidence are capabilities a regulated institution's compliance program may be expected to demonstrate. That is the documented product, and it is a real product. SanctionsAI does not screen adverse media, does not sell analyst tooling, and does not replace a consolidated risk database.

Choose SanctionsAI when the requirement is a sanctions decision in an automated path. The product is a REST endpoint that answers with JSON in real time, with a published price and a free tier that needs no signup or API key. It fits a payment flow, an agent action, or a lightweight onboarding check where the decision is "is this name or wallet OFAC-listed", not "build me a full risk profile".

The two can also be complementary rather than competing: a fast OFAC decision in the hot path, a consolidated-database screen in batch review. That split is supported by the documented delivery methods on both sides - RDC's feeds publish deltas you can mirror, while SanctionsAI answers single checks per call. Whether it is justified is a function of your risk window and audit obligations, not of either vendor's feature list.

How SanctionsAI would evaluate the Dow Jones trial

A trial should test the decision path, not the interface. Use a controlled set of known benign, sanctioned, and ambiguous names your compliance team is permitted to test, and record whether results are explainable and reproducible, how uncertain matches are presented, and what evidence is available for review. Then ask Dow Jones to turn the tested workflow into a written proposal stating the content sets, delivery method, update cadence, usage measure, and all material exclusions.
