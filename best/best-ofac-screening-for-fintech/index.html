The compliance duty your tooling has to serve

Sanctions screening is not optional overhead for a US-connected fintech. OFAC administers civil penalties on a strict liability basis, meaning exposure does not depend on intent, per Treasury's 2019 Framework for OFAC Compliance Commitments. The same framework describes the "50 Percent Rule": entities owned 50 percent or more, individually or in the aggregate, by blocked persons are themselves blocked even if they never appear on the SDN list - which is why tool coverage claims deserve scrutiny.

Two reporting deadlines shape your audit trail. Under 31 CFR 501.603, blocked property and rejected transactions are reported to OFAC within 10 business days. Under 31 CFR 501.604, holders of blocked property file an annual report by September 30 covering June 30 holdings. Whatever tool you buy must produce records that let a compliance officer meet both without archaeology.

The lists themselves are public. OFAC maintains the SDN list and its non-SDN supplements; the UK maintains the OFSI consolidated list for UK-connected activity. Vendors differentiate on how they ingest, deduplicate, match, and explain those lists - not on secret access to them.

What actually differs between tools

Strip the marketing and four dimensions separate candidates:

  • Screening scope: names only, or names plus crypto wallets plus jurisdiction checks; SDN only, or SDN plus non-SDN lists plus the 50 Percent Rule.
  • Integration surface: a REST API your engineers call, batch file feeds for overnight screening, or dashboard-first tooling built for analyst seats.
  • Pricing model: per-check, per-verification, monthly allowance, or quote-based enterprise contracts. Only some vendors publish this.
  • Evidence and audit: whether each result carries a match type, a confidence figure, and a list reference you can put in a 501.603 report.

A concrete way to compare candidates

Demos hide the differences that matter. Run this instead:

  1. Screen your own sample. Take 50 real counterparty names and wallets your compliance team is permitted to test, and push them through every finalist. Generic demo data flatters everyone.
  2. Demand explainable output. Every result should carry a match type, a confidence figure, and the list it matched. Here is SanctionsAI's actual response shape from our docs:

  "matches": [
    {"list": "OFAC_SDN", "match_type": "wallet", "confidence": 1.0}
  ],
  "clean": false,
  "checked_at": 1718000000
}
  1. Check the audit trail against the deadlines. Can you reconstruct, from tool records alone, what was blocked and when - so the 10-business-day 501.603 report is a form rather than a project?
  2. Pin down coverage. Ask each vendor exactly which lists are screened (SDN only, or SDN plus non-SDN lists), whether the 50 Percent Rule is applied, and how updates arrive after Treasury republishes.
  3. Price the real workflow. Ask for the total cost of your expected monthly volume, including what happens above any allowance.

Is OFAC screening required for fintech companies?
Yes. OFAC compliance is mandatory for all US persons and entities, including fintechs. OFAC administers civil penalties on a strict liability basis, so exposure exists even without intent, per Treasury's compliance framework. Payment processors and money transmitters must screen the transactions they move.
Can I integrate OFAC screening via API?
Yes. Most modern screening tools expose REST APIs. SanctionsAI works from a plain curl with no API key on the free tier: GET /sanctions?wallet=0x... or GET /sanctions?name=... returns a clean boolean plus match details, as shown above.
What should a fintech do after a sanctions match?
Halt the payment, then follow the reporting rules: blocked property and rejected transactions are reported to OFAC within 10 business days under 31 CFR 501.603, and holders of blocked property file an annual report by September 30 covering June 30 holdings under 31 CFR 501.604. Your tool's audit trail should support both reports without manual reconstruction.
