Hydra Market OFAC sanctions

Hydra, the world's largest darknet market, was designated by OFAC in April 2022 for facilitating illicit transactions at enormous scale.

TL;DR

TL;DR: OFAC designated Hydra Market in April 2022 for operating a darknet market that facilitated ransomware proceeds, stolen data, and illicit drugs. The designation blocks US persons from any transaction with it under strict liability.

What happened

Hydra operated as a darknet market that ran on the Tor network and accepted cryptocurrency, largely Bitcoin. OFAC designated it in April 2022, the same month German authorities took the platform offline. OFAC designated Garantex alongside Hydra, underscoring that both marketplaces and the exchanges that service them fall within sanctions scope.

The violation cited

OFAC's action treated Hydra itself as a designated entity on the SDN List rather than as a party that paid a civil penalty. Once a market is designated, any US person who transacts with it, even indirectly, is exposed under strict liability. Intent is not required, and civil penalties start at $356,000 per violation. The 50 Percent Rule extends the block to entities 50% or more owned by a blocked person.

The lesson

Markets are not anonymous to enforcement; counterparties are knowable before payment. A clean result returns ALLOW; a flagged result returns BLOCK, which is exactly the control that keeps an agent or platform away from a designated market like Hydra.
