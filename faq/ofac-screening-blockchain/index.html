OFAC Blockchain FAQs

Answers to common questions about OFAC sanctions and blockchain, covering designated addresses and on-chain screening.

TL;DR

TL;DR: OFAC lists wallet and contract addresses on the SDN List, and blockchain payments to those addresses are violations. Screen the address before any on-chain send.

How OFAC touches the chain

Blockchain is a public ledger of addresses, and OFAC designates specific addresses as blocked property. SanctionsAI indexes 947 OFAC-listed crypto wallets across Ethereum, Bitcoin, and Tron, alongside 19,218 SDN names. Because the ledger is pseudonymous, the check must be against the address itself, not a legal name.

Common questions

  • Which chains are covered? The wallet data spans Ethereum, Bitcoin, and Tron, among 16 jurisdictions.
  • How current is the data? It syncs hourly, so newly designated addresses surface quickly.
  • How is screening added? One HTTP call in under 100 ms, returning ALLOW or BLOCK before a payment broadcasts.
