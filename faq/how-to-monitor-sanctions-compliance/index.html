Why monitoring matters as much as screening

A one-time screening check is not a compliance program. OFAC designations change, counterparties change, and the SDN List is updated regularly. Without monitoring, a counterparty that was clean yesterday may be designated today. Ongoing monitoring is what turns a point-in-time check into a durable control, and OFAC strict liability means penalties, starting at over $350,000 per violation, do not wait for you to notice.

What to monitor

Track every screening decision, not just the blocked ones. sanctionsai.dev returns clean (ALLOW) or flagged (BLOCK) in a single HTTP call under 100 ms, and its screening data syncs hourly from the US Treasury sdn.csv, covering 947 OFAC-listed crypto wallets and 19,218 SDN names. Logging each result gives you the raw material for reviews and regulator questions.

A simple monitoring routine

  • Keep a machine-readable log of every screening call and result.
  • Review flagged matches weekly and document the resolution.
  • Set alerts so a new designation triggers immediate re-screening of existing counterparties.

Compliance is a loop: screen, log, review, and re-screen when the list changes.

Treat the log as the program's memory: when a regulator asks why a transaction was allowed, the recorded decision is the answer.
